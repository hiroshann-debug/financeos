🏦 Loan EMI Calculator
Calculate your monthly payment, total cost and interest burden for any loan
📌 How to use Enter the loan amount, the bank's annual interest rate, and tenure in months. Choose Reducing Balance for home/car/personal loans from banks — this is the standard method. Choose Flat Rate only if your lender specifically states it.

Compare 24 vs 36 months — shorter tenure means higher EMI but less total interest.
A good EMI should be below 40% of your monthly income.

📈 Fixed Deposit Calculator
See how much your FD will grow and compare compounding options
📌 How to use Enter the amount you want to deposit and the bank's annual rate. Most Sri Lankan banks compound quarterly — leave that as default.

Check current CBSL-guided rates before placing an FD — rates change frequently.
Higher compounding frequency = slightly higher returns on same rate.
FDs are great for your emergency fund — low risk, guaranteed returns.

🛡️ Emergency Fund Calculator
Find out if your savings can cover a financial emergency
📌 How to use Enter your total monthly expenses — include rent, food, utilities, EMIs, everything. Your wallet balance is pre-filled as current savings.

Keep emergency funds in a liquid FD or savings account — not invested.
Never use credit cards as your emergency fund — that's debt, not safety.
Build this before aggressively paying off low-interest debt.

🏖️ Retirement Planner
Estimate how much you need to save to retire comfortably
📌 How to use The calculator accounts for inflation (6%) to project your future expenses. It then calculates the corpus needed and how much you should invest monthly.

Expected return of 8% is reasonable for a balanced portfolio (equity + FD mix).
Start investing early — retiring at 60 vs 55 makes a massive difference.
Revisit this calculation every 2-3 years as your income grows.

A complete reality check before making any large purchase
📌 How to use Enter the item price and select how you plan to pay. Your income and commitments are pre-filled from your app data.

Golden rule: monthly debt payments should not exceed 40% of income.
Always keep 3 months of expenses in cash after a big purchase.
If verdict is "Wait" — use this to plan a savings timeline instead.
